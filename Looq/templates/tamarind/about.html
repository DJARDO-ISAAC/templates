We start where a community already has a plan

Tamarind began in 2009 with one stove, forty lunches and a group of parents who knew exactly what they needed.

The name

A tree you plant for someone else

A tamarind grows slowly. It can take years to bear its first fruit, and the tree may stand for two hundred more. In many villages, its shade is the first classroom, the meeting room and the market all at once.

We picked the name as a reminder of how we want to work. Patient, rooted in the place, and useful long after the people who planted it have gone. Success, for us, is a program that no longer needs us. In 2021, forty-one schools reached that point.

How we work

Three promises we hold ourselves to

Asked, not assumed

We only begin where a community has invited us in and formed its own committee. No committee, no project.

Local hands

Four in five of our staff live in the regions where they work. We hire locally and buy locally wherever we can.

Open books

We publish our accounts, our results and our failures. If a well breaks, you will read about it here.

Our journey

From one stove to 218 schools

  1. 2009

    One kitchen

    Parents in a Sena Valley village ask for help building a stove. Tamarind begins with one kitchen and forty lunches.

  2. 2013

    Water comes next

    A head teacher points out that lunch needs clean water. The first well goes in, with the first elected committee.

  3. 2017

    Books and nurses

    Reading corners open in Okoro Flats, and the first clinic day runs on the Tessel Coast.

  4. 2021

    Handing over

    Forty-one Sena Valley schools take over the running of their kitchens. We stay on only to help with audit and supply.

  5. 2025

    3.4 million meals

    We now work across 218 schools in four regions, with 180 staff, most of them living where they work.
