Before you buy land

Five checks that save deals.

A site plan is not a title. A fence is not a boundary. These are the checks we run before any peg goes in the ground — and the questions we wish more buyers asked first.

  1. Search the title at the Lands Commission

    A site plan shows where a parcel is. It does not prove who owns it. Search the record and confirm the registered interest before money changes hands.

  2. Match the seller to the record

    Ask for the deed, the probate or the stool and family authority. If the person selling is not the person on the record, find out why — in writing.

  3. Walk the boundary with the neighbours

    Pegs move. Memories do not. Walk the line with the adjoining occupiers and record where each one says the boundary sits before you survey.

  4. Check the planning scheme

    Zoning, setbacks, road reservations and easements can limit what you build even on land you own. Confirm the scheme before you design.

  5. Commission an independent survey

    A surveyor's plan should be signed, dated and referenced to the national grid. An independent survey is the cheapest insurance in a land transaction.

We run these checks as part of every boundary instruction. If you are already in a transaction, bring us in early — before the deposit, not after the dispute.
