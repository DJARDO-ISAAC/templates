The Real Cost of Slow Decision-Making

Mei Lin ZhaoHead of Strategy

Sep 21, 2026•6 min read

Why Speed Matters More Than Certainty

Most leadership teams treat decisions like risks to be minimized — gather more data, run another review cycle, wait for consensus. But every week spent deciding is a week competitors spend acting. In fast-moving markets, the cost of a slow decision often outweighs the cost of an imperfect one. Certainty is expensive, and it's usually not for sale.

Where Decisions Actually Get Stuck

Slow decision-making rarely comes from a lack of information. It comes from structure. Three patterns show up repeatedly in businesses that move too slowly:

  • Too many approval layers: A decision that only needs one signature routes through four, and each layer adds days without adding judgment.
  • Diffused ownership: When a call belongs to "the team" instead of a person, nobody feels safe making it without checking with everyone else first.
  • Fear of being wrong publicly: Leaders delay because a fast wrong call is visible, while a slow right call looks careful — even when it costs more.

How to Build a Faster Decision Culture

Speed isn't recklessness — it's removing the friction that has nothing to do with judgment quality:

  • Set a decision deadline before the discussion starts. A deadline forces the conversation toward a call instead of toward more analysis.
  • Assign one name to every decision. Committees inform; people decide. Keep the two roles separate.
  • Separate reversible from irreversible calls. Most business decisions can be undone. Treat them that way, and move fast. Save the long review cycle for the few that can't be walked back.
  • Reward the decision, not just the outcome. A well-reasoned fast call that doesn't work out shouldn't be punished more harshly than a slow one that quietly avoided the risk.

Conclusion

Businesses don't lose to competitors with better strategies. They lose to competitors who decided faster and adjusted sooner. Building a culture that moves quickly on reversible decisions is one of the few advantages that compounds every single week.
